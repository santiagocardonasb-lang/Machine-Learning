What each cell means in this problem

True Positives — {{ metrics.true_positives }} months. The model said the goal would be reached and it was. The forecast was right and the budget did not need correcting.
True Negatives — {{ metrics.true_negatives }} months. The model said the goal would be missed and it was missed. The warning was correct and the team could react in time.
False Positives — {{ metrics.false_positives }} months. The model announced that the goal would be reached, but it was not. This is the costly mistake: the team stays calm and the month closes below target with no time to correct.

Interpretation of the results

The model classifies correctly {{ "%.1f"|format(metrics.accuracy * 100) }}% of the months it had never seen, which shows it learned a real pattern instead of memorising the training data.

The mistakes concentrate around the border, where the advertising investment is close to the amount needed to reach the goal. That is expected: two months with the same investment can end on opposite sides of the target because of competition, seasonality or price changes, and the model only sees the investment.

For this problem, a false positive is worse than a false negative. Announcing that the goal will be reached when it will not leaves the team without time to react, while a false alarm only leads to spending a little more than necessary. That makes precision the metric to watch, and it currently stands at {{ "%.1f"|format(metrics.precision * 100) }}%.
