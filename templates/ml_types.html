Types of Machine Learning

Machine Learning algorithms are grouped by the kind of information they learn from and by the way they receive feedback about their answers.

Definition

The model is trained with labelled data: every input comes with its correct answer. The algorithm learns the relationship between the two so it can answer for new inputs.

Main characteristics

  • Requires a historical dataset with known results.
  • Its accuracy can be measured by comparing predictions against the real answers.
  • Labelling the data is often the most expensive part of the work.

Problems it can solve

  • Regression: predicting a number, such as sales, prices or temperatures.
  • Classification: assigning a category, such as spam or not spam, fraud or legitimate.

Definition

The data has no labels. The model explores it on its own, looking for hidden structures, groups or anomalies that nobody pointed out in advance.

Main characteristics

  • Needs no labelled data, so it is cheaper to get started.
  • There is no single correct answer to compare against.
  • Its results usually need a human expert to interpret them.

Problems it can solve

  • Clustering: segmenting customers by purchase behaviour.
  • Anomaly detection: spotting transactions that differ from the usual pattern.
  • Dimensionality reduction: simplifying datasets with hundreds of variables.

Definition

An agent learns by trying out actions inside an environment and receiving rewards or penalties. Over many attempts it discovers the strategy that maximizes the reward.

Main characteristics

  • Learns by trial and error, not from a fixed dataset.
  • The feedback can arrive long after the action that caused it.
  • It needs an environment where the agent can practise safely.

Problems it can solve

  • Robots that learn to walk or to grab objects.
  • Game engines such as AlphaGo.
  • Traffic light control and energy management.
