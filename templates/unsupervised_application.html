Clustering Application

K-Means implemented with scikit-learn over {{ info.records }} seismic events.

1. Dataset and justification

Seismic catalogues are a natural case for clustering: every event is described by objective physical measurements, there are no labels saying what type of earthquake it is, and seismology already knows that earthquakes group into populations by depth. That makes it possible to check whether the algorithm recovers a structure that exists independently of it.

Variable Unit Why it was chosen
magnitude Richter scale Measures the energy released. It separates frequent small earthquakes from rare large ones.
depth_km Kilometres The depth of the hypocentre. It is the variable seismology uses to classify earthquakes, and it reflects which tectonic process generated them.

3. Choosing the number of clusters

K-Means does not discover how many groups exist: k has to be decided in advance. Rather than assuming a value, the silhouette score was computed for every k between 2 and 8, and the one that maximises it was selected.

The maximum falls at k = {{ info.best_k }}. The inertia keeps dropping as k grows, which is why it cannot be used on its own to decide: splitting the data into more groups always makes them tighter, even when the extra groups mean nothing.

7. Evaluation: silhouette score

Silhouette score
{{ info.silhouette }}
Inertia
{{ info.inertia }}

The silhouette score measures, for each event, how close it is to its own cluster compared with the nearest neighbouring cluster. It ranges from -1 to 1: values near 1 mean compact and well separated groups, values near 0 mean groups that touch each other, and negative values mean records that were probably assigned to the wrong group.

A score of {{ info.silhouette }} indicates a clear structure. The groups are not perfectly isolated, which is expected: the boundary between shallow and intermediate events is gradual in nature, and a few earthquakes sit between the two populations.
